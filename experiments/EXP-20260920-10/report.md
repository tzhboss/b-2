# Experiment Report — EXP-20260920-10

## Main Result

The task × attribute × reference-frame story extends beyond pitch.

Loudness and speaking rate both show emotion-specific and corpus-dependent Absolute-versus-Relative
preferences, but the pattern is not a copy of pitch. Nine of ten corpus-by-attribute cells have
more than 0.03 range across emotion classes in Relative-minus-Absolute speaker-balanced F1.

This provides strong evidence that reference-frame choice interacts with the acoustic attribute
itself.

## Loudness

MEAD shows a broad Relative-loudness benefit, strongest for neutral and happy. MSP behaves very
differently from pitch: MSP neutral strongly prefers Relative loudness, even though MSP neutral
strongly preferred Absolute pitch.

Thus the same corpus and the same emotion class can prefer different reference frames for
different prosodic attributes.

## Speaking Rate

MSP produces the strongest class split:
- angry strongly favors Relative rate.
- neutral and sad strongly favor Absolute rate.
- adding the stable speaker rate baseline back recovers much of neutral and sad performance.

This mirrors the information-redistribution interpretation: normalization emphasizes within-speaker
state but removes stable speaker-level information that can be useful for particular classes.

## Conflict-Set Boundary

The preregistered fixed conflict thresholds were too sparse for broad cross-attribute confirmation,
especially for rate and MELD. The conflict subset remains informative in well-supported cells, but
the broad conflict-set hypothesis is not claimed as confirmed in EXP-10.

## Decision

mixed: per-emotion cross-attribute heterogeneity and baseline redistribution are supported; broad
fixed-threshold conflict-set generalization is inconclusive.
